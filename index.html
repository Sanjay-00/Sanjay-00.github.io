Table, chart, Excel

The model never touches the arithmetic. It decides what to compute and explains what came back. Python does the rest.

Plans, not generated code

The planner outputs a constrained plan over a fixed business ontology. A compiler turns it into Pandas steps and rejects anything invalid before it runs.

One repair, then stop

If the compiler rejects a plan, the planner gets exactly one retry with the error attached. Bounded retries keep latency and cost predictable.

Fast path with zero LLM calls

Common views are served from pre-computed results. The model is only called when a question actually needs reasoning.

Traced end to end

LangSmith traces every routing decision and stage, so a wrong answer can be traced to the exact step that caused it.

Approach

Deterministic first.
AI where reasoning pays.

The pattern behind every project above. The diagrams show it: most steps are plain Python, and the LLM steps are few and deliberate.

Start with the bottleneck

Understand the workflow, find the recurring delay, and only then choose the architecture. Most bottlenecks are information reaching the wrong person too slowly.

Correctness before intelligence

Business numbers are computed deterministically and validated. AI plans and explains on top of results that are already right.

Measure the friction removed

A project counts when people stop double-checking it. Every tool here is judged by time saved and daily use, not by technique.

If the answer is knowable from the data, it belongs in a table, not a prompt.
A confident wrong answer is worse than a slow right one.
Most "AI projects" are deterministic software with a small reasoning layer on top.
The hardest part of automation is earning enough trust that people stop double-checking.
